    Reference: how to read a residual plot

    How to Read a Residual Plot

    You must always examine the residual plot before trusting any linear regression — even when R² looks impressive. R² can be high while the model is fundamentally wrong. The residual plot reveals what R² hides.

    ✓ Random scatter — model OK
    A shapeless band centred on zero with about the same spread everywhere. Some points sit well above or below the line — that is normal. The linear model is appropriate.
    ✗ Curved pattern — non-linearity
    Residuals are mostly positive at both ends and negative in the middle (or the reverse). The true relationship is curved — try a log, square-root, or polynomial model.
    ✗ Fan shape — heteroscedasticity
    Residuals are tightly bunched at small fitted values and spread out at large ones. Variance is not constant; log-transforming y often fixes this in biological data.
    ✗ Outlier
    One point sits far from all the others. Check it before doing anything else: is it a data-entry error, a measurement problem, or a real biological anomaly?
    The golden rule: Always plot your residuals. A high R² with a curved residual pattern means your model is wrong — it is capturing variance, but with the wrong mathematical form. No single statistic replaces this visual check. The Fish Allometry dataset in this tool demonstrates exactly this problem.

    About This Tool

    This tool helps biostatistics students build an intuitive understanding of linear regression — not just how to run the test, but what the numbers mean and how to judge whether the model is appropriate. Every statistic is computed live in your browser from the data shown. Use the three example datasets to see what good and bad residuals look like, play with the Simulator sliders to change how data are generated, then Upload Your Class Data to analyze measurements you collected. The numbered steps walk through each stage of the analysis.

    Data Description

    • Cricket Chirps — Chirp rate (chirps per 15 s) vs. air temperature (°F) in the snowy tree cricket Oecanthus fultoni, n = 28. Does chirp speed increase linearly with temperature?
    • Plant Growth — Shoot height (cm) vs. nitrogen concentration (mg/L) in Arabidopsis thaliana, 8 nitrogen levels × 4 replicate plants. Does added nitrogen predict plant height?
    • Fish Allometry — Body weight (g) vs. total length (cm) in bluegill sunfish Lepomis macrochirus, n = 36. Can a straight line describe a relationship expected to follow a power law (W ∝ L³)?

    Example datasets are simulated from published biological relationships, with realistic random variation.

    What is a residual?
    The difference between the observed value (y) and the predicted value (ŷ): e = y − ŷ. Positive = above the line; negative = below.
    What does R² mean?
    The proportion of variation in y explained by x. A high R² does NOT guarantee a linear model is appropriate — always check the residual plot.
    SST = SSR + SSE
    Total variation = variation explained by the regression + leftover variation. R² = SSR/SST.
    The p-value for slope
    Tests H₀: slope = 0. A small p-value (< 0.05) is evidence of a linear association in the population. Combine with R² and the residual plots.
